Variable Exposure Delays

A way to decouple two things a single exposure setting normally welds together: how long each guide frame integrates, and how often corrections are sent.

The problem it solves

Modern guide cameras can deliver good star SNR in well under a second, but guiding that often is counterproductive — at sub-second cadence most of what changes between frames is seeing, and the mount gets commanded to chase it. The traditional fix is a longer exposure, but that throws away the short exposure's other benefits (quick star acquisition, fast dither settling, less smearing from periodic error within a frame).

What PHD2 does

With variable exposure delays (Advanced Settings › Camera tab), PHD2 inserts a delay after each guide exposure so the overall correction cadence stays sensible even though each exposure is short. The result behaves like guiding at the combined exposure-plus-delay interval, with each measurement coming from a short, sharp exposure. PHD2's Guiding Assistant recommends a variable delay when your camera and measured conditions suit it — as usual, accepting GA's recommendation is the right default.

Not the same as MinMove: a delay reduces how often PHD2 looks; min-move filters what it reacts to. They complement each other — see Aggressiveness, min-move, exposure.

Reference: PHD2 manual — Advanced settings (Camera tab).

Where this shows up in Ephemeris

Ephemeris detects the exposure-delay setting from the guide-log header and reports it with the session's guide cadence. Observation cards about guide cadence — for instance, very short exposures with no delay on a session that shows seeing-chasing behavior — link here, and a Guiding Assistant recommendation for variable delay found in the log is surfaced verbatim (see The Guiding Assistant).
