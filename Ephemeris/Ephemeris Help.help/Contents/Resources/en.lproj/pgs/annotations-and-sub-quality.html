Annotations and rating your nights

The guide log records what the mount did — not what you did, and not what your imaging frames actually look like. Annotations and sub-quality ratings feed both back into the analysis.

Adding an annotation

In the Library's Recent Nights table, click the Note (+) button on a row, or right-click and choose Add annotation…. The sheet captures:

  • Date — defaults to the night you clicked; adjustable for events between sessions.
  • Label — a short headline, e.g. "OAG damaged and replaced".
  • Categories — multi-select chips: Equipment, Calibration, Environment, Software, or Free text.
  • Detail — as much context as you like.
  • This changed my rig — the rig-mutating flag, below.

Annotations appear as dashed blue vertical lines on the trend chart, as a badge on the night's row, in forum exports, and in Spotlight search results. Either a label or a detail is enough to save.

The rig-mutating flag

Turn on This changed my rig when the event genuinely changed the system — new guide camera, rebuilt imaging train, re-shimmed OAG. It tells the cross-night engine that a new regime starts here: the rig-relative baseline resets from this point forward, so old nights stop dragging on new verdicts.

Categories matter too: an Equipment or Calibration annotation near a detected calibration step-change explains it, suppressing the "Calibration orthogonality shifted" alarm within a few nights of the event. Without the annotation, the engine has to assume the shift was silent and unintended — which is exactly when it should alarm.

Rating your nights: sub-quality verdicts

The guide RMS is a proxy; the imaging frame is ground truth. After examining your subs, rate the night with one of four verdicts:

RoundStars round across the field — the guide RMS reflects reality.
Slightly elongatedMild egg shape — atmosphere or borderline tracking.
TrailedObvious trailing — the guide RMS is lying about the result.
MixedSome frames good, some bad — variable conditions.

When your rating contradicts the log

This is the most valuable signal you can give Ephemeris. If you rate a night Trailed (or Mixed) but the guide RMS was sub-pixel, the engine fires the "Trailed subs despite sub-pixel guide RMS" observation — that gap is the classic differential-flexure signature, which the guide log can't see on its own. The card walks through the usual suspects: OAG prism drift, rotator slip, focuser sag, and mirror flop on SCT-style optics.

Tip: Rate nights even when they're good. A run of confirmed Round verdicts is what lets the star-shape predictor and baseline statistics be trusted when something later goes wrong.
