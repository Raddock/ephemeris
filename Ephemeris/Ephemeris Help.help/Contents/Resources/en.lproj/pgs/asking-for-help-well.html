When to ask for help and how

Forum regulars answer hundreds of "my guiding is bad" posts. The ones that get good answers all contain the same things — and Ephemeris can assemble them for you.

When to ask

Ask once you have a pattern, not a single bad night. One night can be seeing, wind, or a cable; three nights showing the same signature is a question worth other people's time. Work through the alert-severity observations first — if Ephemeris already names the problem and the suggested response, try that before posting.

What a good post contains

  • The rig, with numbers — mount, OTA and effective focal length, both cameras, and crucially both pixel scales. "0.8″ RMS" means nothing until readers know your imaging scale.
  • Guiding Assistant results — measured min-move, backlash, and polar error. These answer half the diagnostic questions before they're asked.
  • Several nights of numbers, not adjectives — RMS per night, what changed between them.
  • The actual guide log — attach the PHD2 .txt file (double-click the night in the Library to find it). Experienced helpers will read it directly.
  • One specific question — "why does Dec spike after meridian flips?" beats "any ideas?".

Let Ephemeris write the boilerplate

In the Library, select the rig and click the Share for help… button in the header (the share icon). The export sheet builds a post containing:

  • Rig header — profile, mount class and model, OTA with reducer math, camera pixel sizes, computed imaging and guide scales.
  • Recent nights — a table of the last seven nights (sessions, integration, median RMS) plus the median/best/worst across the range, expressed against your imaging scale.
  • Active observations — each with its severity and source-authority label intact, so readers can see which claims come from PHD2's own measurements and which are Ephemeris heuristics.
  • Annotations — your recorded equipment and calibration events.
  • Your question — a section for what you actually want to know, with suggested starting points drawn from the data.

Pick the format for the destination

MarkdownGitHub, Discord, Slack, or pasting into a Claude chat.
BBCode (forums)Cloudy Nights and other vBulletin-style forums.
Plain textEmail and mailing lists.

Paste the result, attach the log file, and add anything the data can't show — wind, frost, the cable you bumped.

Tip: If you use Claude, connecting it over MCP skips the copy-paste entirely — Claude reads the same rigs, nights, and observations directly.
