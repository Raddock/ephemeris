Multi-star guiding

Instead of pinning everything on one star, PHD2 can track the primary star plus a set of secondary stars and combine their displacements into one measurement.

Why multiple stars win

  • Noise averaging — each star's centroid carries its own measurement noise. Combining several stars averages it down, improving the effective SNR of every guide frame — typically a visible reduction in reported RMS, especially with faint stars or short exposures.
  • Robustness — a single star can saturate as conditions change, sit on a hot pixel, fade into a cloud edge, or be a close double whose centroid wanders. With multiple stars, one misbehaving star is outvoted instead of steering the mount.
  • Fewer lost-star events — guiding survives one star dropping out entirely.

Using it

Enable Use multiple stars in Advanced Settings › Guiding tab (on by default in current PHD2 2.6.x profiles). PHD2 auto-selects the secondary stars; there is nothing to tune. It quietly falls back to single-star behavior when the field doesn't offer enough usable stars, so there is essentially no downside — this is the modern default and should stay on.

Seeing still applies: multiple stars average out measurement noise, not atmospheric motion — stars across the field move together under seeing. Expect better, not magical, numbers; the seeing floor measured by the Guiding Assistant still stands.

Reference: PHD2 manual — Advanced settings (Guiding tab).

Where this shows up in Ephemeris

Ephemeris reads the multi-star setting from each log header and flags single-star sessions with an observation card suggesting you enable it — an easy, risk-free improvement. Session statistics (see Statistics and drift) are comparable either way, but expect the multi-star sessions in a side-by-side comparison to show modestly lower RMS under the same sky.
