Max RA / Dec duration

A safety ceiling on the length of any single guide pulse, per axis. Useful insurance — until your corrections start hitting it.

What the limits do

Max RA duration and Max Dec duration (Advanced Settings › Algorithms tab, in milliseconds) cap each correction pulse. If the algorithm computes a pulse longer than the cap, PHD2 sends the cap instead. The point is to keep one bad measurement — a cosmic ray, a hot pixel grabbed as the star, a wind gust — from yanking the mount far off target. The deficit is made up over the following frames.

Railed corrections

A frame whose pulse equals the cap is railed (also "rail-limited" or "clipped"): PHD2 wanted to correct more than the limit allowed. Occasional rails after a dither are normal — the settle is supposed to take big steps. Rails scattered through ordinary guiding mean the error is growing faster than the capped corrections can remove it: the mount can't keep up.

If more than a few percent of frames are railed

The limit isn't the problem; the error source is. Check, roughly in order:

  • Polar alignment — large steady drift consumes correction budget continuously.
  • Balance — an axis fighting gravity stalls or surges instead of moving the commanded amount.
  • Wind or vibration — bursts of rails correlated in time, often visible on both axes at once.
  • Cable snags — rails that begin at a particular pointing position and clear after a slew.
  • Dec backlash — rails clustered on direction reversals; see Backlash and uni-directional guiding.
Raising the limit is occasionally right — e.g. a slow guide rate (0.5× sidereal) genuinely needs longer pulses for the same correction. But raise it after ruling out the mechanical causes above, not instead of doing so.

Reference: PHD2 manual — Advanced settings. The chart's Limits overlay (see Reading the guide chart) draws the cap so railed pulses are visible directly.

Where this shows up in Ephemeris

Ephemeris counts railed frames per axis and raises a rail-rate observation card in three tiers: >1% of frames is a watch, >5% a pattern, and >15% an alert. The card names the limiting axis and the configured cap read from the log header.
