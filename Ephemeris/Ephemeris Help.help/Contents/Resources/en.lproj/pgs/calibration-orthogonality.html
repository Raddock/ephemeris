Calibration orthogonality

An equatorial mount's RA and Dec axes are mechanically perpendicular, so the calibration legs PHD2 measures should be too. When they aren't, every guide correction is computed against a skewed coordinate frame.

Why 90° matters

During calibration PHD2 pulses the mount West and North and measures the star's track for each leg. The angle between those two tracks defines the camera-to-mount transform used for every subsequent correction. If the measured legs are not ~90° apart, PHD2 is sending part of each RA correction into Dec and vice versa — corrections fight each other and RMS suffers on both axes.

Reading the error

  • < 5° — normal. Seeing alone produces a few degrees of scatter in the measured angle.
  • 5–10° — suspect. Often a calibration done in poor seeing, too close to the pole, or with declination compensation applied incorrectly (for example, the mount reported a wrong pointing position, so PHD2's cos(δ) adjustment skewed the RA rate).
  • > 10° — something is wrong. Likely causes: severe Dec backlash eating the first North steps, cable drag or mirror shift during a leg, flexure between guide scope and mount, or calibration performed at high declination where RA steps barely move the star.

Fixing it

  • Recalibrate near the celestial equator (Dec ~0°) and near the meridian, where both axes move the star by comparable, well-measured amounts. PHD2's Calibration Assistant slews there for you.
  • If Dec backlash is large, clear it first or run the Guiding Assistant backlash test — see Backlash and uni-directional guiding.
  • If the error persists across calibrations, look for mechanical play; see Calibration troubleshooting.
Don't ignore a repeatable error: one bad calibration is noise; the same 8° skew three sessions in a row is a measurement of your setup.

Where this shows up in Ephemeris

Ephemeris computes the orthogonality error from each calibration in the log as |Δ(west, north)| − 90° and raises a calibration orthogonality observation card when it exceeds the 5° threshold. The same number appears as Ortho error in the Calibration view header.
