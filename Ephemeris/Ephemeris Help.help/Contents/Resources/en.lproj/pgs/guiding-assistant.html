The Guiding Assistant

PHD2's built-in measurement tool. It watches the star with guide corrections disabled, separates seeing from mount behavior, and turns the result into concrete setting recommendations.

What it measures

  • High-frequency RMS (HPF-RMS) — star motion after a high-pass filter removes slow drift. This is the fast, random motion the mount cannot correct: essentially your seeing, as sampled by your guide setup. It is the floor under any RMS number you will ever achieve.
  • RA peak-to-peak — the total excursion in RA, dominated by the mount's periodic error over the sampling window.
  • Drift rates — linear drift in RA and Dec, in arc-seconds per minute.
  • Polar alignment estimate — derived from the Dec drift rate. Approximate; PHD2's drift-align or polar-drift tools are more accurate.
  • Dec backlash (optional) — if you check Measure Declination Backlash, GA drives Dec back and forth and reports the backlash in milliseconds of guide pulse. See Backlash and uni-directional guiding.

How to run it

  1. Connect, start looping, and let PHD2 select a star.
  2. Open Tools › Guiding Assistant and click Start. Guide corrections are suspended while GA samples.
  3. Let it run for at least two minutes — longer is better, especially for the RA peak-to-peak number, which needs to see a meaningful slice of the worm period.
  4. Click Stop, review the recommendations, and click the buttons to apply the ones you accept.

What to do with the recommendations

  • Minimum-move (MinMove) — GA's most valuable output. It sets RA and Dec min-move just above the measured seeing noise so PHD2 stops chasing turbulence. Accept it unless you have a specific reason not to. Details in Aggressiveness, min-move, exposure.
  • Exposure / variable delay — GA may suggest a guide exposure or, on capable setups, short exposures with a variable exposure delay.
  • Drift-limiting exposure — the longest imaging exposure you could take unguided before drift alone exceeds the seeing blur. A small number here is a hint that polar alignment is the limiting factor, not guiding.
  • Backlash compensation — offered when the backlash test measured a correctable amount.
Tip: Re-run the Guiding Assistant after any mechanical change — re-balancing, a new guide scope, meshing the worm — and a couple of times per season anyway. Seeing-derived numbers from last winter don't describe tonight's sky.

Where this shows up in Ephemeris

When a log contains a Guiding Assistant run, Ephemeris surfaces GA's own numbers and recommendations verbatim as a phd2Measurement observation card — the highest-authority tier, since it is PHD2 measuring your mount directly rather than Ephemeris inferring from guide frames. The hygiene strip also tracks GA freshness, nudging you when your last run is old enough that its recommendations may no longer match your sky or rig.
