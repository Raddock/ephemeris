When to recalibrate

A calibration describes the geometry between your guide camera and your mount axes. It stays valid exactly as long as that geometry does — which is both longer and shorter than people tend to assume.

What invalidates a calibration

  • Rotating the guide camera in its holder, even slightly. The camera-to-axes angle is the heart of the calibration.
  • Changing the guide setup — a different guide scope, off-axis guider position, binning, or focal length.
  • Mount re-assembly or transport — tearing down a portable rig and rebuilding it rarely reproduces the old geometry exactly.

What does not require recalibration

  • A meridian flip — if the mount reports its pointing position (an ASCOM/INDI connection that reports pier side), PHD2 flips the calibration automatically.
  • Slewing to a new target — with pointing information, PHD2 adjusts the RA rate for declination on its own.
  • A new night on a permanent setup — this is what auto-restore is for.

PHD2's auto-restore

With Auto restore calibration enabled (Advanced Settings › Guiding tab), PHD2 reloads the last good calibration when the gear connects, so guiding can start without a fresh calibration run. This is the right mode for permanent or pier-mounted rigs — but it also means a quietly invalidated calibration (a bumped guide camera, say) keeps getting restored night after night. The restored calibration's date is the tell: if the log shows a calibration weeks old, make sure nothing has changed since.

Rule of thumb: permanent setup, untouched — recalibrate monthly or when results degrade. Portable setup — recalibrate every assembly. Either way, recalibrate after any hands-on change to the guide camera or its optics.

See also Calibration troubleshooting and Calibration orthogonality. Reference: PHD2 manual — Advanced settings.

Where this shows up in Ephemeris

Ephemeris compares each session's calibration timestamp against the session date and raises a calibration staleness observation card in two tiers: a pattern at 21 days and an alert at 30 days. A stale-but-working calibration isn't an emergency — the card is a prompt to confirm the geometry really hasn't changed.
