Seeing vs equipment — telling them apart

Elevated RMS has two very different causes with two very different fixes. The shape of the noise tells you which one you have.

The seeing signature

Atmospheric seeing is turbulence shifting the star's apparent position many times per second. In the guide data it looks like:

  • Fast, random, frame-to-frame motion — each exposure lands somewhere new with no memory of the last one.
  • No axis preference — the atmosphere doesn't know where your RA axis points. RA and Dec raw distances are similarly noisy, and the scatter-cluster overlay stays round.
  • No structure — no period, no direction, no trend. Worse at low altitude (longer air path) and often accompanied by star-mass / SNR flicker.

PHD2's Guiding Assistant measures exactly this: its high-frequency RMS (the residual after high-pass filtering out everything the mount could plausibly correct) is its seeing estimate, and it derives its min-move recommendations from it. See the Guiding Assistant in the PHD2 manual.

The equipment signature

Mount and mechanical problems are the opposite of random — they have structure:

  • Axis-asymmetric — one axis distinctly worse than the other. The atmosphere can't do that; gears, balance, and backlash can.
  • Periodic — RA error repeating on the worm period is the classic mechanical fingerprint. The FFT periodogram turns a suspicion into a measurement: a spike at the worm period (or a harmonic) is the worm; no spikes plus a broad noise floor is the sky.
  • Directional — steady one-way drift (polar alignment, flexure) or stepwise sticking after reversals (Dec backlash).

Why chasing seeing makes it worse

Seeing motion is gone before any correction can arrive — the star has already moved somewhere else by the next frame. Turning aggression up (or min-move down) to "fix" it makes PHD2 issue corrections for displacements that no longer exist; each pulse becomes a new error the next frame has to undo, and the mount oscillates on top of the seeing. The cure runs the other way: longer guide exposures (2–4 s) to average the atmosphere out, a min-move at or above the seeing noise, and moderate aggression. PHD2's guide-algorithm documentation makes the same point.

Quick test: compare two nights of the same target in the Library. Equipment problems reproduce night after night with the same axis and the same period; seeing varies with the weather and the target's altitude.

Where this shows up in Ephemeris

The recommender's coaching card "Elevated RMS — likely atmospheric, not equipment" fires when high RMS coincides with seeing markers — star-flux variability and/or low target altitude — and deliberately recommends no equipment action. Structured problems surface instead as "Periodic residual detected" (from the periodogram), "Dec corrections heavily one-directional", or "Recent RMS above rig baseline" when nights stop matching your rig's own history. The Star mass / SNR sub-charts and the scatter-cluster overlay in the document window are the places to eyeball the difference yourself. Related: Common patterns and What the guide log can't tell you.
