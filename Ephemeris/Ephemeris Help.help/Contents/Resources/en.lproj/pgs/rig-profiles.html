Rig profiles

A rig profile tells Ephemeris what the guide log can't: the imaging side of your telescope. Open it from the app menu — Ephemeris › Rig Profiles… (⇧⌘,).

Why the imaging scale matters

A PHD2 log only knows the guide camera. Whether 0.8″ RMS is excellent or a problem depends entirely on your imaging pixel scale — 0.8″ is invisible at 2″/px and ruinous at 0.4″/px. The imaging scale anchors:

  • Night verdicts — the sub-pixel / at-resolution / over-resolution coloring across the Library.
  • Star-shape prediction — the per-night chip that says whether stars rendered sharp, bloated, elongated, or trailed.
  • Several observation generators — they stay silent without it.

Until the imaging train is configured, verdicts fall back to rig-relative tiers (Best / Typical / Elevated / Worst, computed against the rig's own history) and the Library shows a banner offering to take you to this window.

Identity: PHD2 name vs display name

The PHD2 profile name is the rig's identity — it comes from the Equipment Profile = … line in each log's header and is the key that matches logs to rigs. It's set when the first log for that profile is opened or imported (rigs are auto-created; you never predeclare them) and it can't be edited, which is why it carries a lock icon.

The display name is purely a label for you — sidebar entries, chart titles, export headers. Leave it blank to fall back to the PHD2 name. If you've renamed the profile inside PHD2 over the years, past names are listed under Past PHD2 names.

Imaging train

OTA focal lengthThe telescope's nominal focal length in mm — without any reducer.
Imaging camera pixel sizeIn microns. This is the imaging camera, not the guide camera.
Binning1×1 through 4×4.
Reducer factorOptional — e.g. 0.7 for a 0.7× reducer. Leave blank if you image at native focal length.

Once these are set, the form shows the computed imaging scale in arc-seconds per pixel (and the effective focal length when a reducer is in play). That single number is what the rest of the app keys on.

Guide train

Pick the configuration — Off-axis guider, Separate guide scope, or Same optics — then the guide focal length, guide-camera pixel size, and guide binning. Unlike the imaging train, these values do appear in the PHD2 log header, so Ephemeris pre-fills them when it auto-creates a rig, and a Fill from open log button appears when a log with usable header values is open.

Mount class

The mount class steers algorithm and tuning advice: Standard gear / belt mount, Encoder-based premium mount, Harmonic strain-wave mount, or Adaptive optics device. The high-precision encoders toggle mirrors the checkbox in PHD2's New Profile Wizard — when on, the recommender biases toward encoder-mount advice such as LowPass2 and Variable Exposure Delays. Advice for harmonic mounts is labeled community consensus, since the PHD2 manual doesn't cover that class.

Tip: Edits here take effect immediately — switch back to the Library and verdicts, chips, and observations recompute against the new scale.
