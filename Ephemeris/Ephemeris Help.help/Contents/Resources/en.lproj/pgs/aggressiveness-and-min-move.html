Aggressiveness, min-move, exposure

Three knobs decide how PHD2 responds to what it sees: how much of each deviation to correct, which deviations to ignore, and how often it looks.

Minimum move — the seeing filter

Deviations smaller than MinMove (in pixels, per axis) produce no correction at all. That is the point: a sub-MinMove deviation is more likely seeing than mount error, and correcting it just injects the atmosphere into your mount. Too low and PHD2 chases seeing; too high and real drift accumulates between corrections.

Don't guess this value — the Guiding Assistant measures your actual seeing noise and recommends MinMove directly. Accept its recommendation and revisit when conditions change markedly.

Aggressiveness — the correction fraction

Aggression is the percentage of the computed deviation that PHD2 actually corrects. At 70%, a 1.0″ error gets a 0.7″ correction. Correcting less than 100% is deliberate: part of any single measurement is seeing, and a full-strength correction overshoots, then overshoots back — visible as oscillation. Raise aggression if errors decay too slowly over several frames; lower it if the axes oscillate around zero.

Exposure — the cadence / SNR tradeoff

Guide exposure sets both how well each measurement averages out seeing and how often corrections can happen. 2–4 seconds suits most setups:

  • Shorter — faster corrections, but each centroid is noisier (lower SNR, less seeing averaged out), so PHD2 reacts more to turbulence. Pair short exposures with a variable exposure delay if you want short-exposure star detection without machine-gun corrections.
  • Longer — smoother, higher-SNR measurements, but the mount can wander further between corrections.
Chasing seeing: the classic symptom is RA and Dec corrections every frame, alternating direction, with RMS no better — often worse — than with corrections disabled. The cure is some combination of higher MinMove, longer exposure, and lower aggression, in that order.

Reference: PHD2 manual — Advanced settings and PHD2 Best Practices. The per-algorithm meaning of these knobs is in Guide algorithms.
