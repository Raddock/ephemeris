Pixel scale and imaging resolution

Every guiding number is meaningless until it's compared against a pixel scale — and it has to be the right pixel scale.

The formula

Pixel scale is how much sky one camera pixel covers, in arc-seconds per pixel:

arcsec/px = 206.265 × pixel_size_µm × binning / (focal_length_mm × reducer_factor)

The constant 206.265 converts radians to arc-seconds (divided by 1000 for the µm→mm units). A reducer factor below 1 shortens the effective focal length and makes the scale coarser; a Barlow does the opposite.

Example — an Edge HD 11 (2800 mm) with a 0.7× reducer and a 3.76 µm camera at bin 1: 206.265 × 3.76 / (2800 × 0.7) ≈ 0.40″/px.

Guide scale vs imaging scale

Your rig has two pixel scales, and they are usually very different:

Guide scaleThe guide camera through the guide optics. PHD2 computes it from the equipment profile and writes it into the log header as Pixel scale = …. Ephemeris uses it to convert the chart between pixels and arc-seconds.
Imaging scaleThe imaging camera through the imaging optics. It never appears in the guide log — you enter it (or its ingredients) in Ephemeris's rig profile.

A short guide scope often runs at 3–5″/px while the imaging train runs at well under 1″/px. PHD2 measures the star to a small fraction of a guide pixel, so a coarse guide scale still measures fine — but a coarse scale can't change what the imaging sensor resolves.

Judge RMS against the imaging scale

Guide RMS is expressed in arc-seconds precisely so it can be compared across cameras — and the comparison that matters is to the imaging scale. A 0.8″ RMS night is excellent on a 2″/px widefield rig and a problem on a 0.40″/px long-focal-length rig: same guiding, different verdict, because the imaging pixels are a different size. That ratio is the subject of The image-scale ratio and round stars.

Seeing sets the floor

Atmospheric seeing typically smears stars to 2–4″ FWHM at most sites, occasionally better at excellent ones. Two consequences:

  • Imaging scales much finer than roughly a third of the seeing FWHM are over-sampled — extra resolution the sky can't deliver. That isn't harmful, but it makes the guiding target proportionally stricter.
  • No mount can guide out seeing. Sub-0.5″ RMS on a typical night is close to the practical floor; chasing lower numbers usually means chasing the atmosphere.
Tip: PHD2's own documentation recommends a guide scale no finer than needed — see the PHD2 manual on equipment profiles and the Guiding Assistant's seeing measurement.

Where this shows up in Ephemeris

You enter focal length, pixel size, binning, and reducer in the rig profile editor; Ephemeris computes the imaging scale with the formula above and uses it everywhere a verdict is shown — the Library's verdict pill, the session chip, and the predicted star shape. The recommender also raises "Guide scale far from imaging scale" when the two scales are badly mismatched, and "Rig profile and PHD2 disagree about the guide scale" when your profile's guide-side numbers don't match the Pixel scale PHD2 wrote in the log header.
