Setting up for the night

Most "bad guiding" is decided before the first frame. Each item below is cheap at dusk and expensive at 2 a.m. — and each one has a fingerprint Ephemeris will flag the next morning if it was skipped.

1. Balance the mount

Balance both axes with the full imaging payload attached — camera, filters, dew strap, cables. Slight east-heavy bias is fine on a gear mount; gross imbalance makes one correction direction fight gravity.

If skipped: expect "Dec corrections heavily one-directional" or "Pulse-rail saturation" — corrections piling up on one side of an axis until they hit the duration limit.

2. Route and dress cables

Loop cables to the mount head, leave slack through the full slew range, and check nothing drags as the scope tracks. A cable snag is a step-change generator: everything is fine until the angle where it isn't.

If skipped: a snag mid-night shows up as a sudden RMS jump, and across nights as "Calibration orthogonality shifted" — the engine sees a mechanical change it can't attribute and asks you to annotate it.

3. Focus the guide camera

Bloated guide stars lower SNR and make the centroid wander. Focus the guide camera as deliberately as the imaging camera — once per season is usually enough on a fixed OAG, but verify after any train change.

If skipped: low star mass and SNR, frame drops, and at worst "Star lost on N% of frames".

4. Calibrate in the right place — when you need to

Calibrate near the intersection of the celestial equator (Dec ≈ 0) and the meridian, where star motion per guide pulse is greatest and the geometry is honest. PHD2's Calibration Assistant slews there and pre-clears Dec backlash for you. You don't need to recalibrate every night — but do recalibrate after rotating the guide camera or changing the train.

If skipped: "Calibration orthogonality error" (axes not measured at 90°), PHD2's own calibration alerts, and the hygiene strip's Calibration pill aging toward red — see Calibration.

5. Run the Guiding Assistant when conditions changed

New site, new season, new equipment, or a long layoff? Give the Guiding Assistant a few minutes before imaging. Its measured min-move, backlash, and polar-error values are the highest-authority numbers in the whole pipeline — they supersede every heuristic Ephemeris computes.

If skipped: the "Guiding Assistant is N days old" / "never been run" hygiene observation, and a GA pill that sits orange or red in the Library.

6. Check polar alignment

A few arc-minutes of polar error is fine for guided imaging; tens of arc-minutes turn into Dec drift the guide loop has to fight all night.

If skipped: Dec-dominated drift in the night's statistics, a trailed star-shape prediction whose tooltip names Dec drift as the cause, and a polar-alignment pill quoting the last GA measurement. See Statistics and drift for the drift-based estimate.

7. Confirm settle thresholds

Make sure your imaging software's dither-settle criteria (pixels and seconds) match what your mount can actually do — a settle threshold tighter than your typical RMS stalls the sequence; one too loose starts exposures while the mount is still moving.

If skipped: long settle events after every dither in the document window's Events card, and elongated first frames after dithers that drag the night's verdict down.

Tip: When something on this list does change — recalibration, rebalance, new cable run — annotate the night. The cross-night engine attributes the change to your note instead of raising an alarm.
