Statistics and drift

Each guiding session is reduced to a small set of numbers. Here is what they mean and how they are calculated.

RMS RA, RMS Dec, Total RMS

Standard deviation of the per-axis raw distance, in pixels (and arc-seconds when a pixel scale is available). Total RMS is the geometric sum:

Total = √(RMS_RA² + RMS_DEC²)

Excluded frames (PHD2 errors and any manual exclusions you've added) are skipped. The frame count shown as X / Y means X included out of Y total.

Caveat: If the log contained values Ephemeris couldn't read (a truncated or corrupted file, usually), they are treated as zero and the inspector shows a Damaged data in this log warning with the count. Statistics for that session may understate the real guiding error. See Reading your guide log.

Peak RA, Peak Dec

The largest absolute deviation on each axis across included frames. A peak much larger than three-times the RMS often points to a single bad frame rather than a guiding problem.

Drift RA, Drift Dec

Linear-regression slope over the session, expressed in arc-seconds per minute (and pixels per minute). A drift larger than ~0.5″/min usually means polar misalignment, mount flexure, or differential refraction.

Polar alignment estimate

Computed from declination drift using König's method:

PA_arcmin ≈ 3.82 × |drift_dec_arcsec_per_min| / cos(δ)

Where δ is the target declination from the session header. The estimate is suppressed near the celestial pole (the cos(δ) term explodes), and it ignores the sign — direction of misalignment cannot be derived from a single session.

Caveat: A polar-alignment estimate from one session is approximate. PHD2's drift-align tool measures two stars near the meridian and equator and is more accurate.

Aggregate statistics (Summary view)

The Summary aggregates per-session RMS using a frame-count-weighted formula:

Aggregate RMS = √(Σ(RMS_i² · n_i) / Σn_i)

This is what you'd get if you concatenated every session's data and computed RMS on the whole stream. Sessions with more frames contribute proportionally more.

Best and worst session

The session with the smallest Total RMS is tagged Best; the largest is Worst. Both are clickable shortcuts to jump straight to that session's chart.
