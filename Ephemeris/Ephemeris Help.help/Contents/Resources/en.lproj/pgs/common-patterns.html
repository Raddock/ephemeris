Common patterns (oscillation, drift, settling)

Most bad guide charts are one of five shapes. Learn the five and the chart starts reading like a diagnosis.

Oscillation — the zig-zag

The trace crosses zero nearly every frame, and the Corrections overlay shows pulses alternating direction: push, overshoot, push back. PHD2 is correcting displacements that were mostly seeing, then correcting its own corrections.

  • Causes: aggression too high, min-move too low, or guide exposures too short for the conditions.
  • Fixes: lower aggressiveness, raise min-move toward the seeing noise, lengthen the exposure to 2–4 s. PHD2's Guiding Assistant will recommend min-move values from a measurement — see Seeing vs equipment.

Steady Dec drift

The Dec line rides a slope, and the Dec corrections are overwhelmingly one direction. The mount's axis isn't pointed at the pole, so targets slide in Dec all night.

  • Causes: polar-alignment error (the drift rate encodes its size); occasionally flexure or refraction.
  • Fixes: re-align. The math and the three PHD2 tools are in Polar-alignment fundamentals.

RA periodic error

The RA line breathes in a slow wave that repeats on a fixed interval — the worm period (commonly several minutes per cycle, depending on the mount). Dec doesn't share it; only RA has a worm driving the tracking.

  • Confirming: the FFT periodogram shows it as a spike at the worm period (and possibly harmonics).
  • Fixes: PHD2's PredictivePEC RA algorithm learns the periodic component and corrects it ahead of time; mounts with built-in PEC training help too. See PHD2's guide algorithms.

Settling failures after dither

Each dither is followed by a long orange settling band, or settling that times out entirely, eating imaging time between subs.

  • Causes: Dec backlash (the mount takes seconds to actually reverse), settle tolerance stricter than the night's seeing, dithers larger than needed, or aggressive corrections re-exciting the star.
  • Fixes: dither in RA only (or smaller), loosen the settle threshold to match conditions, and address backlash — PHD2's Guiding Assistant measures it directly.

Star-lost cascades

Runs of excluded frames, often with Star mass and SNR collapsing first. The events list fills with star-lost messages.

  • Causes: clouds (gradual SNR fade, then loss, then recovery), focus drift or dew on the guide optics (slow star-mass decline all night), or a mount jump / cable snag (instant loss with a position jump on recovery).
  • Fixes: match the cause — weather needs patience, optics need the checks in Optical-train health, jumps need a mechanical inspection.
Tip: shape first, numbers second. RMS tells you how much; the pattern tells you why. Two nights with identical RMS can need opposite fixes.

Where this shows up in Ephemeris

The chart and its overlays are the pattern viewer: the Corrections overlay exposes oscillation and one-sided Dec pulses, settling bands mark dither recovery (orange = failed), and the diagnostics sub-charts catch star-lost cascades before they happen. The recommender turns the patterns into cards: "Dec corrections heavily one-directional" (drift), "Periodic residual detected … try PredictivePEC" (worm), "Star lost on N% of frames" (cascades), and the gap-analysis observations that classify a night's dominant residual. Each card links back to the relevant topic on this page.
