Calibration view

A square XY plot of every calibration step, with reference rings to gauge step distances at a glance.

What you see

  • West / East — RA legs, in red.
  • North / South — Dec legs, in blue.
  • Backlash steps — orange.
  • The origin crosshair shows the lock position.
  • Concentric dashed rings mark round-number distances from origin (1, 2, 5, 10 px and multiples), so you can read step sizes without staring at the axis labels.
  • The endpoint of each leg is annotated with its direction letter (W, E, N, S) and step number.

What good calibration looks like

  • RA and Dec legs roughly perpendicular to each other.
  • Each leg is a clean straight line of evenly spaced points.
  • Return legs (East after West, South after North) overlap the outbound legs.
  • Backlash steps (if shown) are clustered tightly — PHD2 detected and compensated.

Header readouts

Above the plot, a row of stats shows:

  • Device — Mount or AO.
  • Steps — total calibration steps recorded.
  • Started — calibration start time.
  • RA rate — pixels per second along the West leg, parsed from PHD2's calibration completion message.
  • Dec rate — same, for the North leg.
  • Ortho error — the deviation of the West-vs-North angle from 90°. Highlighted in orange when greater than 5°.
If ortho error > 5°: Calibration may have run during high seeing or with mount slop. Re-running calibration near the meridian and the celestial equator usually fixes it.
