The image-scale ratio and round stars

One division turns a night's guide RMS into a prediction about your stars.

The ratio

ratio = Total RMS (arcsec) ÷ imaging pixel scale (arcsec/px)

It answers the only question guiding numbers exist to answer: how far does the mount wander, measured in imaging pixels? A ratio of 1.0 means the guide star wandered by exactly one imaging pixel (RMS). The imaging scale — not the guide scale — is the denominator; see Pixel scale and imaging resolution for why.

The three tiers

Ephemeris buckets the ratio into the same three tiers it shows as a colored verdict pill:

Sub-pixelratio < 0.7 — mount motion is comfortably inside one imaging pixel. Stars stay round; guiding is not your limiting factor.
At resolution0.7 ≤ ratio ≤ 1.0 — motion is about the size of an imaging pixel. Results depend on seeing and sub length: good nights look fine, marginal nights show softness. Amber, not a failure.
Over resolutionratio > 1.0 — motion exceeds an imaging pixel. Symmetric excess blurs stars into soft, bloated discs; axis-asymmetric excess elongates them. Either way the imager can see the guiding error.

Bloated vs elongated

Over-resolution guiding does not automatically mean egg-shaped stars. If RA and Dec wander by similar amounts with no drift, stars stay round but soft — Ephemeris flags these nights with the bloated-star icon rather than predicting elongation. Elongation needs an asymmetry: one axis distinctly worse than the other, or a steady drift in one direction. See Common patterns for what causes each.

Rule of thumb: if you want guiding off the suspect list entirely, aim for sub-pixel (green). At-resolution nights are usually rescued by ordinary seeing blur; over-resolution nights will show up in the subs.
Caveat: a green ratio is a statement about the guide camera only. Round-star guiding with trailed subs points at the optical train, not the mount — see What the guide log can't tell you.

Where this shows up in Ephemeris

These tiers are exactly Ephemeris's verdict colors: the green / amber / coral pill in the Library's verdict column and the session chip in the document window both display Sub-pixel, At resolution, or Over resolution from this ratio (it reads "—" until the rig profile has an imaging scale). The same ratio drives the predicted star shape — including the bloated-star icon on round-but-soft nights — and the recommender cites it in observations such as "Guide scale far from imaging scale".
